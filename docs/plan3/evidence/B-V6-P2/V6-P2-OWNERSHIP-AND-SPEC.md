## 1. Consumer census — the decisive evidence

All three helpers are defined in `chart-indicators-full.js`, which I may not touch. But they are not equally shared. Reference counts, excluding the build-generated mirror and `dist`:

| Helper | Definition | Consumers | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `_isYInMainPricePlot` | `chart-indicators-full.js` ×1 | **`order-manager.js` ×3 — and nothing else** | **Orders-only** |
| `_ensureMainPlotSvgClipDef` | `chart-indicators-full.js` ×1 | **`order-manager.js` ×2 — and nothing else** | **Orders-only** |
| `_getMainPricePlotLayout` | `chart-indicators-full.js` ×21 | `chart.js` ×22, `compare-overlay.js` ×4, `drawing-tools-manager.js` ×4, `talaria-fvg-indicator.js` ×2, `talaria-simple-smc-indicator.js` ×2, `talaria-ratio-gap-indicator.js` ×2 | **Genuinely shared, 7 modules** |

This corrects my earlier report, which treated all three as shared and concluded the whole rule had to move to A. That conclusion was wrong because I measured the aggregate rather than each helper. **Two of the three have exactly one consumer, and it is mine.**

## 2. What that means

A predicate consumed only by orders, living in an indicator module, is not a shared abstraction — it is a **misfiled** one. `_isYInMainPricePlot` decides whether an order's y-coordinate is inside the price plot, and `_ensureMainPlotSvgClipDef` installs the clip path that enforces it. Nothing but orders has ever asked either question.

`_getMainPricePlotLayout` is different and should not move to orders. It answers "where is the main price plot", which seven modules legitimately need. It is chart-layout geometry.

**Proposed seam:**

- `_isYInMainPricePlot` and `_ensureMainPlotSvgClipDef` **move into `order-manager.js`** (Manager B's territory) as order-overlay concerns.
- `_getMainPricePlotLayout` **stays** and becomes an explicitly shared, read-only geometry provider under neutral chart-layout ownership (Manager A). Orders consume it; orders do not define it.

This is a strict improvement: it removes two order-specific behaviours from an indicator module, gives orders control of their own visibility rule, and leaves exactly one genuinely shared function at the boundary.

## 3. The mechanism — and a correction to the original premise

The original framing was that the plot bounds and the `yScale` range numerically disagree. **That premise was overturned.** They agree. The defect is **staleness, not disagreement**: `yScale.range` is recomputed in `calculateScales` (`chart.js` ~26042) while `separateIndicatorPanelHeight` is written elsewhere (`chart-indicators-full.js` ~11331, ~12227, ~12800). When a separate indicator panel opens or resizes, the two are updated in different passes, so for at least one frame the clip is computed against a stale plot height and order lines near the boundary are clipped against the wrong bound.

**This ordering is in `chart.js` and is Manager A's.** It is the part that cannot come back to me regardless of where the helpers live.
