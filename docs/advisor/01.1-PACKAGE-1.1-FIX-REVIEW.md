### 1.1a — Single chart remembers the symbol *and* the timeframe

*This was the main defect in your 1.3 notes: it reverted to the pair the session was created with.*

**Do:**
1. The chart opens on EURUSD. Switch the symbol to **AUDUSD**.
2. Switch the timeframe to **15m**.
3. Close the browser tab entirely. Open a new one, log in, open the same session.

**PASS:** It comes back on **AUDUSD, 15m**.
**FAIL:** It comes back on EURUSD, or on AUDUSD but a different timeframe, or on the timeframe
the session was created with.

> Why it broke: a multichart panel had been writing a session-keyed record carrying only its own
> timeframe, and the merge replaced the whole record rather than its keys — so the host's saved
> symbol was erased. Panels no longer write there at all. Separately, the server's timeframe won
> unconditionally over a newer local one.

**Also do (this is the part that catches a wrong fix):**
4. Now switch to **multichart**, set panel A to GBPUSD, go back to **single chart**.
5. Close the tab, reopen, open the session.

**PASS:** Single chart is still on **AUDUSD, 15m** — the multichart trip did not overwrite it.
**FAIL:** It comes back on GBPUSD, or on EURUSD.

### 1.2a — Refresh returns you to where the playhead actually was

*Your 1.12 note: refresh restored an earlier playhead.*

**Do:**
1. Start replay and let it run for **at least a minute**, so the playhead is clearly past where
   it started. Note the last candle's date and time on the chart.
2. **Pause.**
3. Press **F5**.

**PASS:** Replay comes back at the candle you noted, or within a candle or two of it.
**FAIL:** It comes back visibly earlier — tens of candles, or back at the session start.

**Then do the harder version:**
4. Let replay run another minute. **Do not pause.** Press F5 while it is still playing.

**PASS:** Again within a candle or two of where it was.
**FAIL:** Noticeably earlier.

> Why it broke: the server copy of the playhead is a throttled echo plus a flush on page teardown,
> and a torn-down page can lose that flush — so the server copy was sometimes older than the local
> one, and the server copy always won. Boot now takes whichever of the two is later.

### 1.6a — The first click on an unfocused panel pans it

*Your 1.3 side note: the first click was spent on focus and the drag was swallowed.*

**Do:**
1. Click into panel **A** so it's the focused one.
2. Now put the cursor in panel **C** and, in **one motion**, press and drag sideways. Do not
   click first.

**PASS:** Panel C pans with that drag, from the first press. It also becomes the focused panel.
**FAIL:** The first press only focuses C and the drag does nothing; you have to release and drag
again.

**Repeat for B and D**, each time starting from a different focused panel. All three should pan
on the first motion.

> Why it broke: the guard that asks "is this panel mid-gesture?" read a property no panel record
> has, so it answered "no" for every panel — and the focus fan-out reset the pan about 60ms into
> your drag.

### 1.7a — Clicking between panels does not walk the candles left

*Your 1.3 side note: candles slid left with each focus click until they clamped.*

**Do:**
1. Note where the candles sit in panel A — pick the rightmost candle and remember its time.
2. Click A, B, C, D, A, B, C, D — **eight clicks, fast**, without dragging or scrolling.
3. Look at panel A again.

**PASS:** The rightmost candle is the same one. Nothing drifted in any panel.
**FAIL:** The candles have moved left, in any panel, by any amount.

**Then do:** Ten more rapid clicks around the panels.

**PASS:** Still no drift.
**FAIL:** Drift, or the view eventually jams against an edge as it did before.

> Why it broke: focus republished the range that was already on screen, and each re-pin lost a
> fraction of a bar. Focus now skips the re-pin when the visible range hasn't actually changed.
